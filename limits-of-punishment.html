The Limits of Punishment: Iraq Case Study

Mara Redlich Revkin

Transitional Justice and Violent Extremism. United Nations University and the Institute for Integrated Transitions (May 2018)

This report asks how Iraq should respond to the tens of thousands of people accused of affiliation with the Islamic State. It draws on fieldwork in Baghdad, Erbil, Mosul, a displacement camp near Hawija, and the counter-terrorism court at Tel Kayf; forty-eight interviews with lawyers and law professors, judges and prosecutors, and the wives and mothers of accused members; observation of two trials and of a tribal reconciliation session in Baghdad attended by representatives of thirty tribes from Anbar Province; and a survey of 1,409 residents of Mosul. It argues that a heavily carceral approach to accountability risks entrenching the grievances that allowed the Islamic State to take hold, and sets out alternatives.
