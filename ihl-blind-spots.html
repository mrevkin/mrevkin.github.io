International Humanitarian Law’s Blind Spots: Regulating Military Force Outside Armed Conflict

Mara Redlich Revkin & Orly Maya Stern

Working paper (2026), under review at the International Review of the Red Cross

Abstract

International humanitarian law (IHL) is the primary body of law regulating the conduct of military forces, yet it applies only during armed conflict. How, then, is military conduct regulated when soldiers are deployed in situations that fall outside IHL’s reach? This article identifies three such “blind spots”: peacekeeping operations outside armed conflict, internal military deployments for law enforcement, and extraterritorial detention. It argues that the regulatory frameworks available in IHL’s absence, namely international human rights law, domestic law and rules of engagement, are structurally inadequate substitutes. The article concludes with recommendations, including new ICRC interpretive guidance, standardized rules of engagement for non-combat deployments, stronger accountability mechanisms, specialized training and conditionalities on peacekeeping funding and foreign military assistance.

Keywords: international humanitarian law, peacekeeping, internal military deployments, extraterritorial detention, rules of engagement.
